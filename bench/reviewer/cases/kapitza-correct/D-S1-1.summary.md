## Summary
Wrote derivations/D-S1-1.tex and derivations/D-S1-1.summary.md. Kapitza's separation of slow and fast motions gives an effective potential for the slow angle; its curvature at the inverted position gives the stability condition and the frequency.

## Result
For a pivot oscillating as $a\cos\omega t$ with $a \ll l$ and $\omega \gg \sqrt{g/l}$, the slow angle $\varphi$ moves in the effective potential $U_{\rm eff}(\varphi) = -(g/l)\cos\varphi + (a^2\omega^2/4l^2)\sin^2\varphi$ (per unit $ml^2$). The inverted position is stable if and only if $a^2\omega^2 > 2gl$, and small oscillations about it have angular frequency $\Omega = \sqrt{a^2\omega^2/(2l^2) - g/l}$.

# Task
Find the condition under which the inverted position of a pendulum with a vertically oscillating pivot is stable, and the frequency of small oscillations about it, for small and fast driving.

## Key steps
- The Lagrangian (3) gives the equation of motion (4): the pivot acceleration adds $-a\omega^2\cos\omega t$ to $g$.
- Split $\theta = \varphi + \xi$ (5). The fast part (6) gives $\xi = -(a/l)\cos\omega t\,\sin\varphi$ (7).
- Averaging over one fast period, (8) and (9), gives the slow equation (10) and the effective potential (11).
- The curvature (12) at $\varphi = \pi$ gives $\Omega^2$ (13), the stability condition (14) and the frequency (15).

## Assumptions
- Rigid massless rod, point mass, no friction.
- $a \ll l$ and $\omega \gg \sqrt{g/l}$, so that the slow and fast motions separate and $\xi$ stays small.
- $\varphi$ is treated as constant over one period of the fast motion, and terms of second order in $\xi$ are dropped.

## Sanity checks
- Dimensions: $g/l$ and $a^2\omega^2/l^2$ are both inverse times squared, so (13) is homogeneous.
- Without driving ($a \to 0$): $\Omega^2 \to -g/l$, the ordinary inverted pendulum, which is unstable.
- Lower position: $U_{\rm eff}''(0) = g/l + a^2\omega^2/(2l^2) > 0$, so it stays stable for any driving, with a raised frequency.
- The condition (14) reads $\omega/\sqrt{g/l} > \sqrt{2}\,l/a \gg 1$, consistent with the fast-driving assumption.
