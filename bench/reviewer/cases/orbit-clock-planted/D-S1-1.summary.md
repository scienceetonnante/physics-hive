## Summary
Wrote derivations/D-S1-1.tex and derivations/D-S1-1.summary.md. The radial geodesic equation gives the orbital angular velocity; the clock rate then follows from the gravitational shift of a static observer at the same radius and the time dilation of the clock's speed relative to that observer.

## Result
A clock on a circular geodesic orbit at Schwarzschild radius $r$ around a mass $M$ runs at $d\tau/dt = \sqrt{(1 - 2GM/(rc^2))(1 - GM/(rc^2))}$ relative to the coordinate time $t$ (the proper time of static observers at infinity). Along the way, $\Omega^2 = (d\phi/dt)^2 = GM/r^3$ exactly.

# Task
Find the rate $d\tau/dt$ of a clock on a circular geodesic orbit in the Schwarzschild metric, as a function of the orbital radius.

## Key steps
- Radial geodesic equation (2) with the Christoffel symbols (3) on a circular orbit gives (4) and Kepler's law $\Omega^2 = GM/r^3$ (5).
- A static observer at the same radius has the proper time (6) and measures the length (7) along the orbit, hence the speed (8) and (9).
- Time dilation in the observer's local frame (10), combined with (6), gives the result (11).

## Assumptions
- Vacuum Schwarzschild exterior; the clock is a test body moving on a geodesic.
- Circular equatorial orbit. Circular timelike geodesics exist only for $r > 3GM/c^2$, and are stable for $r > 6GM/c^2$.
- The static observer's frame is locally inertial over the short time the clock takes to pass, so special-relativistic time dilation applies in it.

## Sanity checks
- Dimensions: $GM/(rc^2)$ is dimensionless.
- $r \to \infty$: $d\tau/dt \to 1$.
- Weak field (12): $d\tau/dt \simeq 1 - GM/(rc^2) - v^2/(2c^2)$ with $v^2 = GM/r$, the sum of the gravitational and the Newtonian kinematic shifts.
