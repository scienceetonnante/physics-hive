## Summary
Wrote derivations/D-S1-1.tex and derivations/D-S1-1.summary.md. Method of images: the grounded-sphere image plus a compensating charge at the centre, then the Coulomb force of the two images on the point charge.

## Result
A point charge $q$ at distance $d > R$ from the centre of an isolated, uncharged conducting sphere of radius $R$ feels the force $F = -q^2R(2d^2 - R^2)/[4\pi\varepsilon_0\, d^3(d^2-R^2)^2]$ along the line of centres (negative means towards the sphere). The sphere's potential is $q/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 d)$.

# Task
Find the electrostatic force on a point charge placed outside an isolated, uncharged conducting sphere, as a function of its distance from the centre.

## Key steps
- Grounded-sphere image $q' = -qR/d$ at $b = R^2/d$ (1); the distance relation (2) shows that the surface potential vanishes (3).
- A second image $q'' = -q'$ at the centre (4) restores zero total charge and keeps the surface an equipotential, at the potential (5).
- The force of the two images (6), with the terms (7), gives (8); combining the fractions (9) gives the result (10).

## Assumptions
- Electrostatics in vacuum outside the sphere; perfect conductor.
- The sphere is isolated and its total charge is zero; uniqueness for a conductor of given total charge makes the image solution the solution.

## Sanity checks
- Sign: attractive for all $d > R$ and for either sign of $q$, as expected for a neutral conductor.
- $d \to R^+$: the force diverges, as the charge approaches its image.
- $d \gg R$: the force decreases as $d^{-5}$, the power law of a charge interacting with the dipole it induces.
- The force depends on $q^2$ only, so it is unchanged when $q \to -q$.
