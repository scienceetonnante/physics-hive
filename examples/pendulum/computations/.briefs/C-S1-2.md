# Brief for C-S1-2

## Target

Check, independently, the following statement about the simple pendulum (point mass on a massless rigid rod of length $L$, no friction, uniform gravity $g$):

> The period of the simple pendulum at amplitude $\theta_0$ is $T = T_0\,(1 + \theta_0^2/16 + O(\theta_0^4))$ with $T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$, so $c_2 = 1/16$.

Do it in two ways:

1. **Symbolic.** Starting from the equation of motion in `conventions.md`, obtain the exact period $T(\theta_0)$ as an expression you can expand, and expand $T/T_0$ in powers of $\theta_0$ with SymPy. Report $c_2$ and $c_4$ as exact rationals.
2. **Numerical.** Evaluate the exact period at several amplitudes spanning small to large values (for instance $\theta_0$ from about $0.01$ to $3$ rad), by two independent routes: the closed form in terms of the complete elliptic integral of the first kind, and a direct numerical integration of the equation of motion (no elliptic function involved). Compare both with the second-order formula $T_0(1 + c_2\theta_0^2)$ and report the relative error at each amplitude. Check that the residual $T/T_0 - 1 - \theta_0^2/16$ scales as $\theta_0^4$ at small amplitude, and extract $c_2$ from the numerical data alone (for instance from $(T/T_0 - 1)/\theta_0^2$ as $\theta_0 \to 0$).

Say which convention (modulus $k$ or parameter $m = k^2$) each library call uses. Save the table of amplitudes, periods and relative errors as a data file.
