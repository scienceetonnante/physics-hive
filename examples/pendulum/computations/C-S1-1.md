# Task
For the simple pendulum of `conventions.md`, compute the coefficient a in T(θ₀) = T₀(1 + a θ₀² + O(θ₀⁴)), with T₀ = 2π√(L/g), working in units where ω₀ = 1 (so T₀ = 2π). The brief asks for four things:

1. Obtain a symbolically from an exact expression of the period, and give the θ₀⁴ coefficient too.
2. Evaluate the exact period numerically by two independent paths: the complete elliptic integral (mpmath and SciPy), and integration of θ'' + sin θ = 0.
3. Extract a from the numbers, by the limit of (T/T₀ − 1)/θ₀² and by a polynomial fit with an error estimate.
4. Tabulate the relative error of T₀(1 + aθ₀²) at θ₀ = 0.1, 0.2, 0.5 and 1.0 rad.

The brief agrees with `conventions.md`: modulus k = sin(θ₀/2), parameter m = k², and both `scipy.special.ellipk` and `mpmath.ellipk` take m.

## Computation

**The exact period.** Energy conservation, θ'²/2 − cos θ = −cos θ₀ (with ω₀ = 1), gives T = 4∫₀^θ₀ dθ / √(2(cos θ − cos θ₀)). Writing cos θ − cos θ₀ = 2(k² − sin²(θ/2)) and substituting sin(θ/2) = k sin φ turns this into T = 4K(k), so T/T₀ = (2/π)K(k). The script checks this identity numerically before using it: it evaluates the θ-integral directly with mpmath at 40 digits, rewritten as θ = θ₀ − u² to remove the endpoint singularity, and compares it with 4K at θ₀ = 0.001, 0.1, 0.5, 1, 2 and 3 rad. The two agree to 10⁻⁴⁰ relative.

**Symbolic path (SymPy).** Three routes:
- (1a) `series((2/pi)*elliptic_k(sin(theta0/2)**2), theta0, 0, 8)`.
- (1b) Expand (1 − m sin²φ)^(−1/2) to order m³, integrate each term over φ ∈ [0, π/2], check the coefficients against ((2n)!/(2²ⁿ n!²))², then substitute m = sin²(θ₀/2) and expand in θ₀.
- (1c) Lindstedt–Poincaré: write τ = ωt, θ = ε cos τ + ε³θ₃(τ) and ω² = 1 + ε²ω₂, keep the θ³/6 term of sin θ, and choose ω₂ to cancel the secular cos τ term at order ε³. This route does not use the period integral at all.

**Numerical path, elliptic integral.** T/T₀ = (2/π)·ellipk(m) with m = sin²(θ₀/2), evaluated with mpmath at 40 digits and with SciPy in double precision, at 12 amplitudes from 10⁻³ to 1 rad.

**Numerical path, equation of motion.** SciPy `solve_ivp` (DOP853, rtol = 10⁻¹³, atol = 10⁻¹⁵) integrates u'' = −sin(θ₀u)/θ₀ from u(0) = 1, u'(0) = 0, where u = θ/θ₀. Rescaling by θ₀ makes the tolerances independent of the amplitude. The period is the time between the first two downward zero crossings of u, located by event detection at t = T/4 and t = 5T/4. No elliptic-integral formula is used.

**Extraction of a.**
- (3a) Richardson extrapolation in x = θ₀² of g(θ₀) = (T/T₀ − 1)/θ₀², using mpmath values at θ₀ = 0.2/2ʲ for j = 0 to 5.
- (3b) Least-squares fit of T/T₀ − 1 = a x + b x² + … with no constant term, at 25 amplitudes in [0.05, 0.5] rad, for polynomial degrees 2 to 5 in x. The fit is done separately on SciPy data, mpmath data and ODE data. The data are deterministic, so the least-squares standard error underestimates the truncation bias. The quoted error is therefore the larger of that standard error and the change in a between degree 4 and degree 5.

## Agreement
The three symbolic routes give the same a = 1/16. Routes 1a and 1b also give the same θ₀⁴ and θ₀⁶ coefficients; route 1c, carried only to order ε², checks only a. The numerical paths agree with 1/16 at the level of their own accuracy:
- Richardson extrapolation on 40-digit mpmath values: to 1×10⁻²³.
- Fit on the elliptic-integral data: to 2.6×10⁻¹² (quoted error 3.5×10⁻¹⁰).
- Fit on the ODE data, which uses no elliptic integral: to 3.2×10⁻¹² (quoted error 3.5×10⁻¹⁰).

Between the two numerical paths for the period itself, the ODE period agrees with the elliptic value to better than 1×10⁻¹⁴ relative at every tested amplitude. The integrator carries a small systematic offset of about +8×10⁻¹⁵ in T/T₀. This offset sets the floor of the ODE-only estimate of g at the smallest amplitude (θ₀ = 10⁻³ gives g − 1/16 = 1.05×10⁻⁸), which is why a is extracted from fits over [0.05, 0.5] rad rather than from the smallest amplitudes. The ODE period is stable: the full period equals four times the quarter period to 8×10⁻¹⁵, and loosening the tolerance from 10⁻¹³ to 10⁻¹¹ changes it by less than 10⁻¹².

## Sanity checks
- **Small-amplitude limit.** At θ₀ = 10⁻⁸, T/T₀ − 1 = 6.25×10⁻¹⁸, which equals θ₀²/16.
- **Known closed form.** At θ₀ = π/2, (2/π)K = Γ(1/4)²/(2π^{3/2}) = 1.180340599016096226 to all 19 printed digits; the ODE gives 1.180340599016107.
- **Shape of T(θ₀).** T/T₀ increases monotonically on (0, π). It diverges logarithmically as θ₀ → π: at π − θ₀ = 10⁻⁶ it equals (2/π) ln(8/(π − θ₀)) = 10.119041 to 8 digits.
- **Modulus and parameter.** Passing k instead of m = k² to `ellipk` would give g(0.01) = 12.5 instead of 0.0625, with T/T₀ − 1 growing linearly in θ₀. The correct convention is the only one compatible with a finite a.
- **Dimensions.** T₀ carries the time units. T/T₀ depends only on the angle θ₀, so a is a pure number per rad². In degrees it would be (π/180)²/16.
- **Higher order.** The fitted θ₀⁴ coefficient matches 11/3072 to 1.5×10⁻¹⁰. The error of T₀(1 + θ₀²/16) matches −(11/3072)θ₀⁴ at small θ₀, to 0.4% at 1 rad.
- **Expansion variable.** The m-series coefficient 9/64 of k⁴ is not the θ₀⁴ coefficient. With k² = sin²(θ₀/2) = θ₀²/4 − θ₀⁴/48 + …, the θ₀⁴ coefficient is 9/1024 − 1/192 = 11/3072. The θ₀² coefficient is the same in both forms: (1/4)(θ₀/2)² = θ₀²/16.
