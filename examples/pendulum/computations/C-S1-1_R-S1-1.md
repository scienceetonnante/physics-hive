## Result
I re-ran `C-S1-1.py`, and it reproduced the `.out` file and both CSV files byte for byte. I then checked the result by four routes that the computation does not use. First, a symbolic expansion of the energy integral after the substitution θ = θ₀ sin ψ, without the elliptic substitution, gives 1 + θ₀²/16 + 11θ₀⁴/3072 + 173θ₀⁶/737280 + 22931θ₀⁸/1321205760. Second, Gauss's formula T/T₀ = 1/AGM(1, cos(θ₀/2)), extrapolated with Richardson at 60 digits, gives a − 1/16 = −7×10⁻³⁹ and b − 11/3072 = −7×10⁻⁴⁰, where b is the θ₀⁴ coefficient. Third, a 30-digit Taylor integration of θ'' + sin θ = 0 (mpmath) at θ₀ = 0.1, 0.2 and 0.4 rad matches 1/AGM to all 25 printed digits. After subtracting the θ₀², θ₀⁴ and θ₀⁶ terms, its remainder scales as θ₀⁸ with the expected coefficient. Fourth, I redid the Lindstedt–Poincaré secular condition by hand. The dimension, small-amplitude limit and parity checks all pass. The two claims are established separately. CL-S1-1 (a = 1/16) is supported by three symbolic routes, one of which never uses the period integral, and by two numerical paths. CL-S1-2 (11/3072) is supported by two symbolic routes, by fits of b to both the elliptic-integral data and the ODE data, and by the θ₀⁶ scaling of the error of the order-4 formula. My remarks are all cosmetic.

## Remarks
- [cosmetic] `C-S1-1.md`, table "Numerical, selected rows", and `C-S1-1.periods.csv`: `ratio_mp` receives the amplitudes as Python floats, so the 22- to 30-digit values of T/T₀ are for the binary double nearest each decimal amplitude, not for the decimal value itself. At 0.1 rad the CSV gives 1.000625358307736634047, while the exact amplitude 0.1 gives 1.000625358307736633977, a difference of 7×10⁻²⁰. This has no effect on a or b: the Richardson extrapolation uses exact decimal amplitudes, and the fits are in double precision.
- [cosmetic] `C-S1-1.md`, "Extraction of a": the quoted uncertainty b = 0.0035807290 ± 1.8×10⁻¹¹ is the least-squares standard error alone. The same file says this error underestimates the truncation bias, and the actual deviation from 11/3072 is −1.5×10⁻¹⁰, about eight standard errors. The rule used for a (the change between degrees 4 and 5) would give about 1.4×10⁻⁸ for b. The value of 11/3072 rests on the symbolic routes, so the claim is unaffected.
- [cosmetic] `C-S1-1.md`, header: `supports` lists only CL-S1-1, although the computation also derives and checks the θ₀⁴ coefficient stated in CL-S1-2. This review records both claims.

## Verdict
Confirmed. **CL-S1-1 is established by this computation.** Three symbolic routes give a = 1/16: the SymPy series of K, a term-by-term integration, and Lindstedt–Poincaré. The last one does not use the period integral, and I verified it by hand. Two numerical paths agree with 1/16: Richardson extrapolation from the elliptic integral (to 10⁻²³), and a fit to ODE periods computed without any elliptic integral (to 3×10⁻¹²). **CL-S1-2 is also established by this computation.** Routes 1a and 1b give 11/3072, and the script checks their common starting point, T = 4K(k), against the direct energy integral at 40 digits. Fits to the elliptic-integral data and to the ODE data recover b ≈ 0.0035807. The error of the order-4 formula scales as θ₀⁶ with the coefficient 173/737280. Lindstedt–Poincaré, as carried out here, does not reach this order. My own checks confirm both coefficients by routes the computation does not use: a direct expansion of the energy integral, Gauss's AGM formula to 39 digits, and a 30-digit Taylor integration of the equation of motion. No test failed, and every remark is cosmetic.
