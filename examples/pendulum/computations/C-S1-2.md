# Task
Check, independently, that the period of the simple pendulum at amplitude $\theta_0$ is $T = T_0\,(1 + \theta_0^2/16 + O(\theta_0^4))$ with $T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$, so $c_2 = 1/16$. The brief asks for two paths:

- **Symbolic.** Starting from the equation of motion, obtain the exact $T(\theta_0)$ and expand $T/T_0$ with SymPy, reporting $c_2$ and $c_4$ as exact rationals.
- **Numerical.** Evaluate the exact period at amplitudes from about 0.01 to 3 rad by two routes: the closed form with $K$, and a direct ODE integration. Compare both with $T_0(1 + c_2\theta_0^2)$. Check the $\theta_0^4$ scaling of the residual, and extract $c_2$ from the numerical data alone.

The brief agrees with `project.md` (goal) and `conventions.md`: the equation of motion is $\ddot\theta + \omega_0^2\sin\theta = 0$, $\theta_0$ is the maximum angle, and $K$ is written with the modulus $k$.

## Computation
All periods are given as the dimensionless ratio $T/T_0$; equivalently $\omega_0 = 1$ and $T_0 = 2\pi$. To get a time in seconds, multiply by $2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$.

**Symbolic (SymPy 1.14).**

- **S1. Energy integral.** SymPy checks that the energy $\tfrac12\dot\theta^2 - \omega_0^2\cos\theta$ is conserved on the equation of motion. This gives $\dot\theta^2 = 2\omega_0^2(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)$, and by symmetry $T = 4\int_0^{\theta_0} d\theta/|\dot\theta|$.
- **S2. Substitution.** With $\sin(\theta/2) = k\sin\phi$ and $k = \sin(\theta_0/2)$, SymPy reduces the integrand to $1/(\omega_0\sqrt{1-k^2\sin^2\phi})$. Literally, it returns the ratio of the two integrands as $\cos\phi/|\cos\phi|$, which equals 1 on $[0,\pi/2]$; it is also equal to 1 to 18 digits at 6 random points. Hence $T = (4/\omega_0)K(k)$ and $T/T_0 = (2/\pi)K(\sin(\theta_0/2))$.
- **S3. Series of $K$.** $K$ is expanded in the parameter $m=k^2$ by integrating the binomial series of the integrand term by term. The result is identical to `sympy.elliptic_k(m).series(m)` (parameter $m$) and to $\tfrac{\pi}{2}\sum_n\big[\binom{2n}{n}/4^n\big]^2 m^n$.
- **S4. Expansion in $\theta_0$.** The series of S3 is evaluated at $m = \sin^2(\theta_0/2)$ and expanded in $\theta_0$ through order 8. The odd coefficients vanish.
- **S5. Lindstedt–Poincaré, with no elliptic integral.** With $\tau = \omega t$, $\Omega = \omega^2 = 1 + a_2\epsilon^2 + a_4\epsilon^4 + a_6\epsilon^6$ and $\theta = \epsilon\cos\tau + \epsilon^3y_3 + \epsilon^5y_5 + \epsilon^7y_7$, the initial data are $\theta(0)=\epsilon$ and $\dot\theta(0)=0$, so that $\theta_0=\epsilon$ exactly. Requiring no secular term at each order fixes the $a_{2j}$. Then $T/T_0 = \Omega^{-1/2}$.

**Numerical (NumPy 2.4, SciPy 1.17, mpmath).**

- **Closed form**, $T/T_0 = (2/\pi)K$, evaluated three ways:
  - `mpmath.ellipk(m)` at 40 digits, which takes the **parameter** $m=\sin^2(\theta_0/2)$; this is the reference value;
  - `scipy.special.ellipk(m)`, which also takes the **parameter** $m$;
  - a pure-NumPy AGM, $K(k) = \pi/(2\,\mathrm{AGM}(1, k'))$, which takes the complementary **modulus** $k' = \cos(\theta_0/2)$.
- **Direct ODE integration.** The equation integrated is $u'' = -\sin(\theta_0 u)/\theta_0$, with $u = \theta/\theta_0$, $u(0)=1$ and $u'(0)=0$. The integrator is `scipy.integrate.solve_ivp` with DOP853, rtol $10^{-13}$ and atol $10^{-15}$. The period is computed twice, from event location: as 4 times the first zero of $u$, and as 2 times the time of the turning point at $u=-1$.
- **Amplitudes:** 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3 rad.

## Agreement
- **Symbolic, two routes.** The elliptic-integral series (S3–S4) and the Lindstedt–Poincaré expansion of the equation of motion (S5) agree exactly through $\theta_0^6$; the difference is identically 0. The series from SymPy's `elliptic_k` also agrees exactly.
- **Symbolic against numerical.** The truncated series matches the mpmath exact value with a relative error that shrinks by the expected order:
  - at $\theta_0=0.1$: $-3.6\times10^{-7}$ at order 2, $-2.3\times10^{-10}$ at order 4, $-1.4\times10^{-16}$ at order 8;
  - at $\theta_0=0.5$: $-2.2\times10^{-4}$, $-3.7\times10^{-6}$ and $-1.4\times10^{-9}$ at the same orders.
- **Numerical routes.** scipy, the AGM and mpmath agree to within $1.3\times10^{-15}$. The ODE integration (quarter period) agrees with mpmath to within $1.3\times10^{-14}$ at every amplitude. The half-period estimate agrees to within $1.6\times10^{-14}$, except $9.4\times10^{-13}$ at 3 rad, close to the separatrix. These differences are at least 6000 times smaller than the relative error of the second-order formula at every amplitude (the smallest of those errors is $3.6\times10^{-11}$, at 0.01 rad), so the comparison with the second-order formula gives the same result against either route.
- **$c_2$.** The value fitted from numerical data alone, 0.0625000033, matches the exact $1/16$ to within $3.3\times10^{-9}$.

## Sanity checks
- **Small amplitude.** At $\theta_0 = 10^{-8}$, mpmath gives $T/T_0 - 1 = 6.25\times10^{-18}$, which equals $\theta_0^2/16$.
- **Scaling of the residual.** The log–log slope of $T/T_0 - 1 - \theta_0^2/16$ against $\theta_0$ on $[0.01, 0.3]$ is 4.0014 from the mpmath data and 4.0014 from the ODE data. Residual$/\theta_0^4$ tends to $c_4 = 11/3072 \approx 0.0035807$.
- **Near the separatrix**, $\theta_0\to\pi$. $T/T_0$ approaches $(2/\pi)\ln(4/\cos(\theta_0/2))$: 3.348496 against 3.348203 at 3.1 rad, and 7.2358741 against 7.2358741 at 3.1415 rad. The period diverges logarithmically, as expected.
- **Sign.** $c_2 > 0$: larger swings are slower. The second-order formula underestimates $T$ at every amplitude tested.
- **Dimensions.** $T/T_0$ is dimensionless, and the mass drops out at the first step (S1).
