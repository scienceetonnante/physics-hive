## Result
I re-ran the script from a copy outside the workspace. It reproduces `C-S1-2.out` line for line and `C-S1-2.periods.csv` byte for byte. I then checked $c_2 = 1/16$ by routes that the script does not use, and all of them pass. The first expands $(2/\pi)K$ as the hypergeometric series ${}_2F_1(\tfrac12,\tfrac12;1;\sin^2(\theta_0/2))$; it gives $1/16$, $11/3072$, $173/737280$, $22931/1321205760$ and $1319183/951268147200$, the standard values through $\theta_0^{10}$. The second expands the period integral $4\int_0^{\theta_0}d\theta/\sqrt{2(\cos\theta-\cos\theta_0)}$ directly, with no elliptic function; it gives $1/16$, $11/3072$ and $173/737280$. The third is a 50-digit tanh-sinh quadrature of that same integral at the 13 amplitudes, which matches the CSV's mpmath column to within $5\times10^{-20}$, the precision of the stored strings. The fourth is an implicit Radau ODE integration in the original variable $\theta$, which matches to within $2.7\times10^{-15}$. A 3-point Richardson fit on quadrature data at 0.001, 0.002 and 0.004 rad gives $c_2 = 0.0625000000000000000$ to 20 digits and $c_4 = 0.00358072916667 = 11/3072$. I also recomputed the CSV's derived columns (relative error of the second-order formula, residual$/\theta_0^4$) independently, and they agree. The checks of dimensions, evenness in $\theta_0$, the $\theta_0\to0$ limit and the $\theta_0\to\pi$ logarithmic limit all pass. I found no remark that weakens or breaks the result. The only remark is cosmetic: a stale flag in the artefact about the letter $m$.

## Verdict
Confirmed. The computation establishes $T = T_0(1 + \theta_0^2/16 + O(\theta_0^4))$, so $c_2 = 1/16$, by two symbolic routes and several numerical routes. The symbolic routes are the elliptic-integral series and a Lindstedt–Poincaré expansion that uses no elliptic integral. The numerical routes are mpmath, SciPy, the AGM and a direct ODE integration. The script re-runs and reproduces its recorded output and data exactly. My independent checks reach $1/16$ by routes the script does not use: the hypergeometric series, a direct expansion of the period integral, 50-digit quadrature and an implicit ODE solver. They also confirm $c_4 = 11/3072$ and the higher coefficients against standard values. Since the series is analytic and even in $\theta_0$, the $O(\theta_0^4)$ remainder is justified. The only remark is cosmetic.
