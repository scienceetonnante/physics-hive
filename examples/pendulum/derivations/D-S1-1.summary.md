## Summary
Wrote derivations/D-S1-1.tex and derivations/D-S1-1.summary.md. Energy conservation gives the exact period as a complete elliptic integral; expanding K(k) and k = sin(θ₀/2) gives c₂ = 1/16, which an independent Poincaré–Lindstedt calculation confirms.

## Result
For the simple pendulum $\ddot\theta + \omega_0^2\sin\theta = 0$ with amplitude $\theta_0 \in (0,\pi)$, the exact period is
$$T(\theta_0) = \frac{2T_0}{\pi}\,K\bigl(\sin(\theta_0/2)\bigr) = 4\sqrt{L/g}\;K\bigl(\sin(\theta_0/2)\bigr),$$
with $K$ written with the modulus $k$, as in `conventions.md`. Its expansion is
$$T(\theta_0) = T_0\Bigl(1 + \tfrac{1}{16}\theta_0^2 + \tfrac{11}{3072}\theta_0^4 + O(\theta_0^6)\Bigr),$$
so $c_2 = 1/16$ exactly (and $c_4 = 11/3072$). Assumptions: point mass, massless rigid rod, no friction, uniform gravity, libration ($\theta_0 < \pi$), $\theta_0$ in radians.

# Task
For the simple pendulum (point mass on a massless rigid rod of length $L$, no friction, uniform gravity $g$), derive analytically the period $T(\theta_0)$ to first non-trivial order in the amplitude: find $c_2$ in $T(\theta_0) = T_0(1 + c_2\theta_0^2 + O(\theta_0^4))$, $T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$, as an exact number, and state the exact expression of the period if the derivation goes through one.

## Key steps
- Energy conservation gives $\dot\theta^2 = 2\omega_0^2(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)$ (eq. 3). Parity and time-reversal symmetry make the period four times the quarter-period integral (eq. 4).
- The half-angle identity with $k = \sin(\theta_0/2)$ (eqs. 5–6) and the substitution $\sin(\theta/2) = k\sin\phi$ (eq. 7) turn the integral into $T = (4/\omega_0)K(k)$ (eqs. 8–9).
- The binomial series of the integrand, integrated term by term with the Wallis integrals (eqs. 10–11), gives $K(k) = \frac{\pi}{2}\sum_n [\binom{2n}{n}4^{-n}]^2 k^{2n}$ (eq. 12), hence $T/T_0 = 1 + k^2/4 + 9k^4/64 + \dots$ (eq. 13).
- Substituting $k^2 = \theta_0^2/4 - \theta_0^4/48 + \dots$ (eq. 14) gives $c_2 = 1/16$ (eq. 15), $c_4 = 11/3072$ (eq. 16) and $c_6 = 173/737280$ (eq. 17), collected in eq. 18.
- Independent check (§3): Poincaré–Lindstedt on $\sin\theta \approx \theta - \theta^3/6$. Removing the resonant term gives $\omega^2 = \omega_0^2(1 - A^2/8)$, so $T = T_0(1 + A^2/16)$ (eqs. 19–21). The amplitude $A$ equals $\theta_0$ because the solution has a turning point at $\theta = A$.

## Assumptions
- The model of the brief: point mass, massless rigid rod, no friction, uniform $g$, planar motion.
- $0 < \theta_0 < \pi$, so the motion is a libration, $k = \sin(\theta_0/2) < 1$, and the integrable singularity at $\theta = \theta_0$ is of the form $(\theta_0 - \theta)^{-1/2}$.
- Term-by-term integration of the binomial series is justified by uniform convergence on $[0, \pi/2]$, since $k^2 < 1$.
- In the Poincaré–Lindstedt check, the standard assumption that the frequency and the solution have regular expansions in the amplitude.

## Sanity checks
- $\theta_0 \to 0$: $K(0) = \pi/2$ gives $T \to T_0$. Passed.
- $\theta_0 \to \pi$: $K \to \infty$ logarithmically, so $T \to \infty$, as expected when the pendulum approaches the upright equilibrium. Passed.
- Sign: $c_2 > 0$, as expected, because $\sin\theta < \theta$ makes the restoring force weaker than in the linear problem, so the period grows with amplitude. Passed.
- Parity: the equation of motion is invariant under $\theta \to -\theta$, and only even powers of $\theta_0$ appear. Passed.
- Dimensions: $c_2$ is a pure number, the prefactor $\sqrt{L/g}$ is a time, and the mass drops out. Passed.
- Two methods (the exact period and Poincaré–Lindstedt) give the same $c_2 = 1/16$. Passed.
- Hand evaluation at $\theta_0 = 0.1$: the series for $K$, summed through $k^8$, gives $(T/T_0 - 1)/\theta_0^2 = 0.06253583$. The truncated expansion $1/16 + (11/3072)\theta_0^2 + (173/737280)\theta_0^4$ gives the same value to eight digits. Passed.
