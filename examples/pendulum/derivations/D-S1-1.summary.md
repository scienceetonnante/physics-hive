# Task
For the simple pendulum of `conventions.md`, derive the exact rational coefficient a in T(θ₀) = T₀ (1 + a θ₀² + O(θ₀⁴)), with T₀ = 2π√(L/g). If feasible, use two independent analytic routes and say whether they agree. State where the period comes from, which expansion is made, and why the neglected terms are O(θ₀⁴).

## Key steps
- **Route 1 (exact period).** Energy conservation gives θ'² = 4ω₀²(k² − sin²(θ/2)) (Eqs. 3–4). The four quarter-swings take equal times, so T = 4 ∫₀^θ₀ dθ / θ' (Eq. 5). The substitution sin(θ/2) = k sin φ turns this into T = (4/ω₀) K(k) = (2/π) T₀ K(k) (Eqs. 6–7). Expanding the integrand binomially and using Wallis integrals gives K = (π/2) Σ cₙ² k²ⁿ, with cₙ = C(2n,n)/4ⁿ, that is (π/2)(1 + k²/4 + 9k⁴/64 + …) (Eqs. 8–10). The remainder after the k² term is bounded by C·k⁴ (Eq. 11). With k² = θ₀²/4 − θ₀⁴/48 + … (Eq. 12), T/T₀ = 1 + θ₀²/16 + O(θ₀⁴) (Eqs. 13–14).
- **Route 2 (Lindstedt–Poincaré).** Set τ = ωt and u(τ) = θ, with u(0) = ε and u'(0) = 0, so that θ₀ = ε exactly (Eq. 16). The symmetry u → −u makes ω even in ε. Expand ω² = ω₀²(1 + λε² + …) and u = εu₁ + ε³u₃ + … (Eqs. 17–19). At order ε, u₁ = cos τ (Eq. 20). At order ε³, u₃'' + u₃ = (λ + 1/8) cos τ + (1/24) cos 3τ (Eq. 21). Removing the secular term requires λ = −1/8 (Eq. 22), which leaves u₃ = (cos τ − cos 3τ)/192 (Eq. 23). Then ω = ω₀(1 − θ₀²/16 + …) and T = T₀(1 + θ₀²/16 + …) (Eqs. 24–25).
- **Agreement.** Both routes give a = 1/16 (Eq. 26).

## Assumptions
- The ideal pendulum of `conventions.md`: point mass, massless rigid rod, uniform g, no friction, planar motion, equation θ'' + ω₀² sin θ = 0.
- The pendulum is released from rest at θ₀ with 0 < θ₀ < π. It therefore oscillates, and θ₀ is both the turning angle and the maximum of |θ|.
- Route 1: the binomial series is integrated term by term. This is justified because it converges uniformly for k < 1 and all its terms are non-negative.
- Route 2 is a formal perturbative expansion; it shows that the ε³ coefficient is consistent but does not bound the remainder by itself. The O(θ₀⁴) remainder rests on route 1 (Eq. 11 and the analyticity argument after Eq. 14).

## Sanity checks
- **θ₀ → 0:** K(0) = π/2, so T → T₀. This holds in both routes.
- **θ₀ → π:** k → 1 and K diverges logarithmically, so T → ∞, as expected for a release near the upright position. A positive a is consistent with this growth.
- **Sign:** sin θ < θ, so the restoring torque is weaker than in the linear problem and large swings are slower. This requires a > 0, which holds.
- **Dimensions:** θ₀ is in radians, so a is a pure number, and T₀ has dimensions of time.
- **Symmetry:** the potential is even in θ, so T is even in θ₀ and no θ₀³ term can appear. Route 1 shows this through analyticity in k² = sin²(θ₀/2), and route 2 through the parity of ω(ε).
- **Hand evaluation at θ₀ = 0.5:** summing the series for K gives T/T₀ ≈ 1.0158525, while 1 + θ₀²/16 = 1.015625. Adding the by-product term (11/3072)θ₀⁴ leaves a residual of 3.7×10⁻⁶ ≈ 2.4×10⁻⁴ θ₀⁶, which is consistent with an O(θ₀⁶) remainder. This is a rough check only; the systematic numerical check belongs to the separate computation.
