## Result
I checked the derivation with tests that have a clear outcome, and all passed. The dimensions are consistent. The limits $\theta_0 \to 0$ and $\theta_0 \to \pi$ behave as expected, and the result is even in $\theta_0$, as the symmetry $\theta \to -\theta$ requires. A sympy series of $(2/\pi)K(m=\sin^2(\theta_0/2))$ reproduces $c_2 = 1/16$, $c_4 = 11/3072$ and $c_6 = 173/737280$ exactly, along with the intermediate series of eqs. (Kseries) and (k2). Direct quadrature of the time integral (eq. Tint) agrees with $4K/\omega_0$ to 15 digits for $\theta_0$ from 0.1 to 3.0. In the high-precision exact period, $(T/T_0 - 1)/\theta_0^2$ tends to $0.0625$ as $\theta_0 \to 0$, and the residual scales as $(11/3072)\theta_0^2$. A direct ODE integration of $\ddot\theta + \sin\theta = 0$ gives the same period to about $10^{-12}$, independently of the elliptic-integral route. The particular solution in the Poincaré–Lindstedt check (§3) and the right-hand side of eq. (PL3) are correct. The derivation's hand evaluation at $\theta_0 = 0.1$ matches the exact value to the digits quoted. No remark weakens or breaks the claim.

## Flags
- The derivation states correctly that `mpmath.ellipk` and `scipy.special.ellipk` take the parameter $m = k^2 = \sin^2(\theta_0/2)$, not the modulus. The numerical comparison that `project.md` asks for (a separate computation) should be checked for this point, because passing $k$ instead of $k^2$ is the most likely error there.
