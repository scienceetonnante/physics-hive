## Question

How to build approximations to the finite-amplitude period from the standard series of the exact period, with error bounds, for amplitudes up to $90^\circ$.

## Method

Starts from the energy integral written as $T = T_0\,(2/\pi)\int_0^1 [(1-z^2)(1-x^2z^2)]^{-1/2}\,dz$ with $x = \sin(\theta_0/2)$ (his Eqs. 5–6), which is $T = (2T_0/\pi)K(x)$ with $x$ the modulus. He expands $(1-x^2z^2)^{-1/2}$ binomially and integrates term by term, obtaining the coefficients through recurrence relations. Truncations of the series give lower bounds on the period. Replacing the dropped terms by a geometric tail gives upper bounds, and intermediate formulas are interpolated between the two.

## Main result

- Series of the exact period (his Eqs. 16–18): $T/T_0 = \sum_{n\ge0} c_n x^{2n}$ with $c_0 = 1$ and $c_n = (1 - 1/2n)^2 c_{n-1}$, so $T/T_0 = 1 + x^2/4 + 9x^4/64 + \dots$ with $x = \sin(\theta_0/2)$. This is the standard Maclaurin series of $(2/\pi)K(x)$.
- The paper does **not** write the expansion in powers of $\theta_0$. Substituting $x^2 = \sin^2(\theta_0/2) = \theta_0^2/4 - \theta_0^4/48 + \dots$ gives $T/T_0 = 1 + \theta_0^2/16 + 11\theta_0^4/3072 + \dots$ (my one-line substitution, not stated in the paper).
- Table I gives the exact $T/T_0$: 1.004301 ($15^\circ$), 1.017409 ($30^\circ$), 1.039973 ($45^\circ$), 1.073182 ($60^\circ$), 1.118959 ($75^\circ$), 1.180341 ($90^\circ$). I reproduced all six values to the digits shown with `mpmath.ellipk` (which takes the parameter $m = x^2$).
- Approximation formulas: for example $T \approx T_0\,[1 + (x^2/4)/(1 - 3x^2/5)]$ (his Eq. 24), with relative error below 0.035% up to $75^\circ$. He also lists the Kidd–Fogg, Molina–Parwani, Lima–Arun and Ganley formulas with their errors.

## Assumptions

Ideal simple pendulum released from rest at amplitude $\theta_0$; amplitudes up to $90^\circ$ are the focus.

## Conventions

The modulus is called $x = \sin(\theta_0/2)$ (our $k$). $T_0$ is the small-amplitude period, as in `conventions.md`.

## Relevance

This paper complements Lima and Arun (2006). It gives the explicit series of the exact period from which $c_2 = 1/16$ follows in one line, and a table of exact values usable as an independent check of a numerical evaluation of $K$. Status: an arXiv preprint marked "Submitted to Amer. J. Phys., July 2, 2007"; I found no evidence that it was published, so it is weaker as a citation than as a working reference. For the series the paper cites Abramowitz and Stegun, *Handbook of Mathematical Functions* (Dover, 1968), and J. B. Marion, *Classical Dynamics of Particles and Systems*, 2nd ed. (Academic Press, 1970), which it says gives the truncated series "that often appears in textbooks". I read the PDF (there is no LaTeX source on arXiv).
