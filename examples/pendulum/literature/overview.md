# Overview of the literature

## Exact period and its expansion in the amplitude

The result the project targets is classical, and the papers agree on it. Energy conservation gives the period of the ideal pendulum released from rest at $\theta_0$ as a complete elliptic integral of the first kind, $T = 4\sqrt{L/g}\,K(k) = (2T_0/\pi)\,K(k)$ with modulus $k = \sin(\theta_0/2)$ (Lima and Arun 2006; Gatland 2007). Expanding $K$ gives $T/T_0 = 1 + k^2/4 + 9k^4/64 + \dots$, with the recurrence $c_n = (1-1/2n)^2 c_{n-1}$ for the coefficients (Gatland 2007). In powers of the amplitude this becomes $T = T_0(1 + \theta_0^2/16 + 11\theta_0^4/3072 + \dots)$. The truncation $T_0(1+\theta_0^2/16)$ is stated explicitly by Lima and Arun (2006), who attribute it to Bernoulli (1749). They take this attribution from a 1960 textbook by C. J. Smith, which I did not read. Lima and Arun state the formula without deriving it; Gatland (2007) gives the series in $k$ but not in $\theta_0$, so the step to $1/16$ is a one-line substitution, done in this survey. Gatland's Table I lists exact values of $T/T_0$ at $15^\circ$ to $90^\circ$, which I reproduced with mpmath. These values can serve as an external check of the project's numerical evaluation. Lima and Arun note that the relative error of $T_0(1+\theta_0^2/16)$ exceeds 0.1% above about $41^\circ$. Both papers cite textbooks for the exact result: Thornton and Marion's *Classical Dynamics*, and Abramowitz and Stegun. The NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions states the period as $4K(\sin\frac{\alpha}{2})$ in units with $g/L = 1$ (§22.19(i)), and the Maclaurin series of $K(k)$ in the modulus (eq. 19.5.1); I read both online. One convention trap appears in this literature: some papers write $K$ with the parameter $m = k^2$ under the same letter. Libraries do the same (`scipy.special.ellipk`, `mpmath.ellipk`).

## Closed-form approximations at large amplitude

Many recent papers on the pendulum period, mainly in teaching journals, propose closed-form formulas that stay accurate up to $90^\circ$ or beyond. Examples are the Kidd–Fogg formula $T_0/\sqrt{\cos(\theta_0/2)}$, Molina's $T_0(\sin\theta_0/\theta_0)^{-3/8}$, Ganley's formula, Lima and Arun's logarithmic formula, and truncated series corrected for the dropped terms; Lima and Arun (2006) and Gatland (2007) compare them. Expanding each of these formulas gives $1 + \theta_0^2/16$ at second order (I checked this in this survey). They matter for the project only as checks; the question is the second-order coefficient, which none of them disputes.
