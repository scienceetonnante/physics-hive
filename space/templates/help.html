How this works

PhysicsHive lets researchers steer AI coding agents on one problem in theoretical physics or mathematics. A project has two roles. The contributor runs the sessions: the agents work in a terminal, on the contributor's machine. The lead sets the question, reads what the agents found and answers them, and decides what enters the draft. One person can be both the lead and the contributor. This site, the Space, is where the lead reads and answers; on this page, "you" means the lead.

The cycle

  1. You write the question and the goal on the Project page, and the next steps if you have them.
  2. The contributor runs /session-run in a terminal. The session proposes a brief, by default the first next step, and the contributor confirms it. The session then works on its own for a few hours: it reads papers, derives and computes, and has every result reviewed blindly by another agent.
  3. It comes back with a short note in LaTeX and a debrief: questions for you, and lines to tick. Each line is the exact change that the next session will make if you tick it.
  4. On the Review page, you answer the questions, tick the lines you accept, write anything else in the note box, and save.
  5. The next session applies the lines you ticked before anything else. The Space applies nothing by itself.

The pages

  • Review: the session waiting for your response, with its note on the left and your response on the right. Under each result, a link opens its evidence: the derivation, the computation and the reviews.
  • Project: project.md (the question, goal, approach and next steps) and conventions.md (notation, units, signs).
  • Draft: draft.tex, the best current state of the research, as a draft paper. Only the lead decides what enters it.
  • Sessions: every session, each on its page with its note, your response and its record.
  • Literature: the papers the agents found, by topic. Your ratings guide the next survey.

What "established" means

A claim is established when a result of the draft names it. A result needs a claim with a confirming review of a derivation or a computation, or a check by the lead; any other claim goes in the draft as a conjecture.
